Frequently Asked Questions

Clear, direct answers for Dealer Principals, CFOs, and Service Directors regarding why dealerships need audits, tangible financial benefits, non-disruption, and data confidentiality.

Section 1

Why Dealerships Need Audits & Expected ROI

1. Dealer Principal / Client ko After-Sales Audit kyu karana chahiye? +

Automobile workshops operate in high-velocity environments managing hundreds of repair orders and thousands of spare parts line items daily. In this complex flow, revenue leakages silently accumulate across multiple blind spots:

  • Parts issued from store but dropped or unbilled on final tax invoices.
  • Technicians performing labor operations that are never added to the repair order billing.
  • Manual discounts given at cash counters without GM/Dealer Principal authorization.
  • Rejected OEM warranty claims left un-refiled and accumulating in suspense accounts.
  • Physical spare parts theft or shrinkage disguised through negative stock adjustments.
  • Vehicles driven out through the security gate without verified invoice settlement.

Internal workshop managers are occupied with daily customer delivery pressures and OEM CSI targets. They lack the independent bandwidth and forensic data tools to audit their own floor peers. An independent audit brings objective truth, stops silent bleeding, and restores 4% to 9% of net after-sales profit.

2. Audit se dealership ko kya fayada aur measurable ROI milta hai? +

Dealership after-sales audits deliver both immediate cash recovery and permanent structural cost savings:

  • Immediate Cash Recovery (30 Days): Recovery of unbilled receivables, re-filing of legitimate rejected OEM warranty claims within cutoff limits, and recovery of unbilled customer parts.
  • 3x to 7x First-Year ROI: The quantifiable revenue leakage identified and arrested typically equals 3 to 7 times the total cost of the audit engagement.
  • Inventory Capital Optimization: Identification of dead stock (>180 days) for OEM return programs, eliminating holding costs and freeing tied-up working capital.
  • Control & Deterrence: When staff know that DMS logs, parts requisition slips, and gate passes are independently audited, internal manipulation drops to near zero.

In short: The audit pays for itself many times over while protecting the Dealer Principal’s business equity.

3. How does this audit differ from statutory financial audits or OEM audits? +

There is a fundamental difference in purpose, scope, and allegiance:

  • Statutory CA Audits: Focus on general ledger verification, GST/tax filing, and balance sheet accuracy. They do not inspect workshop job cards, technician bay times, oil dispenser calibration slips, or physical parts bins.
  • OEM Brand Audits: Carried out by the manufacturer to verify brand standard compliance, tool certifications, and warranty claims eligibility. OEM audits often seek clawbacks and disallowances against the dealership!
  • Our After-Sales Consulting Audit: Operates 100% in the dealership's financial interest. We scrutinize the micro-operational ground realities that statutory auditors overlook and defend your revenue from OEM clawbacks and internal leakage.

Section 4

Practice Details: Counter Sales & RSA Audits

9. Why is a dedicated Counter Sales (OTC) Audit critical for parts profitability? +

Over-the-counter (OTC) retail parts sales—such as engine oil, battery replacements, tyres, and accessories—often bypass workshop repair order controls. Without specialized scrutiny, common leakages occur:

  • Cash collections received from walk-in customers or independent mechanics without generating a DMS tax invoice.
  • Scrap battery core values kept off the books instead of credited to the dealership.
  • Unauthorized trade discounts or credit extended to external garages without management approvals.
  • Parts stock recorded as sold on credit notes that are later cancelled without inventory return.
10. What specific leakages are uncovered in Roadside Assistance (RSA) Audits? +

Roadside Assistance involves third-party towing vendors, OEM roadside portals, and emergency call logs. Our audit verifies:

  • Whether towing vendor invoices match actual GPS trip logs and agreed per-kilometer rate slabs.
  • Whether customer RSA memberships (free OEM period vs paid extension) were verified before dispatching free towing.
  • Whether reimbursement claims were submitted to the OEM within portal cutoff deadlines to prevent claim expiration.
  • Whether towing services provided to non-policy vehicles were correctly billed to the vehicle owner or customer invoice.
