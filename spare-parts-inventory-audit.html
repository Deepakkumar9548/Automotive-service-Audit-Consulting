Spare Parts & Inventory Audit

Independent physical stock count, DMS reconciliation, unbilled consumption scrutiny, scrap bin governance, and non-moving inventory analysis.

Practice Overview

Protecting Working Capital & Eliminating Parts Shrinkage

Spare parts inventory typically represents 60% to 75% of total after-sales billing and carries the dealership's largest working capital exposure. Discrepancies between parts issued from the store and parts actually billed on customer invoices represent one of the single biggest sources of unrecovered revenue leakage in automotive dealerships.

Our Spare Parts & Inventory Audit provides end-to-end reconciliation: from Goods Received Notes (GRN) to physical bin placement, technician requisition slips, job-card consumption, and final tax invoice generation. We detect unauthorized stock adjustments, scrap pilferage, and capital trapped in obsolete inventory.

Strategic Imperative

Why Dealerships Need This Audit

Spare parts inventory represents 30% to 50% of dealership working capital. Physical shrinkage, negative stock balances, unrecorded scrap parts, stock aging past 180 days, and unposted Goods Receipt Notes (GRN) create massive hidden balance sheet write-offs that erode profitability.

Common Ground Blind Spots Uncovered:
  • Physical parts missing from store bins while showing as active in DMS inventory.
  • Negative stock balances created by storekeepers to bypass stockout warnings.
  • Lakhs of rupees locked in obsolete and non-moving parts (>180 days).
  • High-value parts (ECUs, sensors, headlights) vulnerable to internal pilferage.
Comprehensive Scope

What We Audit

  • Physical vs System Stock: Wall-to-wall or statistical ABC sampling comparing physical bin counts against DMS stock ledgers.
  • Parts Consumption Verification: Matching store issue slips with corresponding job card billings to verify 1:1 consumption.
  • Unbilled Parts Consumption: Identifying parts physically issued to floor technicians but omitted from the final invoice.
  • Parts Return & Cancellation: Scrutiny of credit returns back to store without supervisor approval or physical part return.
  • Slow & Non-Moving Stock: Ageing categorization (>90 days, >180 days, >365 days) and obsolescence risk quantification.
  • Stock Adjustment Verification: Investigation into manual positive/negative stock write-offs and ledger overrides.
  • Inter-Branch Parts Transfers: Verification of Stock Transfer Notes (STN) and Delivery Challans between dealership branches.
  • Scrap & Replaced Part Control: Inspection of scrap bins to ensure replaced customer parts are physically accounted for.
Risk Exposure

Common Inventory Vulnerabilities We Uncover

Critical Inventory Red Flags Found in Dealerships:

  • Discrepant Issue Slips: High occurrence (e.g. hundreds of cases worth ₹30L+) where parts charged on customer invoices do not match the original store requisition slips.
  • Technician Handover Gaps: Parts handed across the store counter without technician physical sign-off or digital acknowledgment.
  • Ghost Stock Adjustments: Unexplained negative stock write-offs performed at month-end to conceal physical bin shortages.
  • Lube & Consumables Leakage: Engine oil, coolant, and brake fluid billed in fractional quantities with no physical dipstick reconciliation.

Procedure

Our Inventory Audit Procedure

1
Physical Count Execution

Surprise physical bin audits covering 100% of 'A' category parts (high-value aggregates, ECUs, turbochargers) and sample 'B' & 'C' consumables.

2
Issue-to-Invoice Match

Automated script-based reconciliation comparing every store issue slip row against the billed part line-items on the tax invoice.

3
Adjustment Scrutiny

Filtering every manual inventory adjustment entered during the quarter, verifying written management authorization and reason documentation.

4
Inter-Branch Transfer Reco

Tracking transit status of parts dispatched from main dealer store to satellite workshops to prevent in-transit shrinkage.

5
Dead Stock Categorization

Stratifying non-moving inventory by age to recommend OEM stock returns, inter-dealer liquidation, or scrap disposal.
