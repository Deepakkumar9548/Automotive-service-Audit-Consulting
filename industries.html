Automotive Segments & Dealership Profiles

Tailored audit protocols calibrated to the specific business models, OEM franchise mandates, and operational velocities of distinct automotive businesses.

1. Passenger Vehicle (PV) Dealerships

Mass & Premium Car Franchise Networks

Passenger vehicle dealerships handle massive daily vehicle turnover, extensive customer-facing service advisory desks, and fierce competition for technician retention. With complex OEM quarterly schemes, free service coupons, and intensive bodyshops, margin leakage commonly hides within customer discounts, unbilled washing, and slow insurance claim settlements.

Key Audit Focus Areas

Pre-invoice scrutiny, sub-₹100 invoices, unbilled value-added services (car detailing/coating), CSI feedback authenticity, and quick service bay turnaround times.

Business Impact

Immediate 2–4% recovery in service gross margins, protection of OEM CSI quarterly bonuses, and complete physical gate control.

2. Commercial Vehicle (CV) Dealerships

Truck, Bus, Tipper & SCV Operations

Commercial vehicle workshops operate on high-value parts aggregates, major fleet annual maintenance contracts (AMC), mobile service vans, and substantial credit lines granted to transport operators. Uncollected credit customer balances, high-ticket warranty aggregate claims, and bulk lubricant leakage represent immediate cashflow hazards.

Key Audit Focus Areas

Fleet debtor scrutiny (>90 days), engine/transmission warranty aggregate disallowances, mobile service van inventory tracking, and scrap barrel reconciliations.

Business Impact

Accelerated recovery of outstanding fleet receivables, defense against massive OEM aggregate chargebacks, and tight bulk oil control.

3. Multi-Brand Automotive Dealer Groups

Corporate Groups Operating Across Multiple OEMs & Territories

Managing 5 to 50+ workshop locations across different OEM brands (e.g. Maruti, Hyundai, Mahindra, Tata, Toyota) creates enormous governance complexity. With different DMS systems, differing regional practices, and autonomous branch heads, dealer group directors frequently lack unified visibility into systemic operational risk and group-wide inventory stagnation.

Key Audit Focus Areas

Inter-branch benchmarking, normalized labor absorption analytics, central parts warehouse transfers, group cashier banking compliance, and standardized DOA policies.

Business Impact

Unified executive dashboard across brands, closing inter-branch operational loopholes, and safeguarding corporate balance sheets.

4. OEM-Authorised Service Centres (ASC)

Standalone Authorised Workshops & 2S Facilities

Standalone 2S (Service & Spares) workshops operate under intense OEM compliance audits, mandatory special tool investments, and stringent warranty claim documentation rules. Being purely service-driven, any billing leakage or inventory loss cuts directly into enterprise survival.

Key Audit Focus Areas

OEM standard operational readiness, defective warranty scrap disposal evidence, technician certifications tracking, and store issue slip matching.

Business Impact

100% OEM franchise audit readiness, avoiding manufacturer penalties, and maximizing workshop bay revenue per hour.

5. Multi-Outlet Independent Workshop Networks

Organized Aftermarket Chains & Fast-Fit Networks

Organized multi-brand aftermarket garage chains rely on rapid turnaround, standardized pricing menus, and high parts gross margins. However, without OEM-mandated DMS controls, independent workshops face rampant cashier pilferage, unauthorized aftermarket part substitutions, and informal cash handovers.

Key Audit Focus Areas

Cashier daily banking reconciliations, OEM vs OES part purchase verification, digital gatepass enforcement, and job card estimate compliance.

Business Impact

Institutional financial governance, eliminating unauthorized cash transactions, and enabling scalable franchise growth.
