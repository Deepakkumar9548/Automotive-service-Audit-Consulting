Workshop Operations Audit

Independent scrutiny of floor velocity, repair order lifecycles, technician time clocking, bay productivity, and work-in-progress (WIP) bottlenecks.

Practice Overview

Maximizing Workshop Velocity & Shop Floor Discipline

In an automotive workshop, operational friction directly translates into lost revenue and dissatisfied customers. When repair orders sit open for weeks, bays remain idle due to poor scheduling, or technicians fail to log actual labor hours, the dealership's fixed absorption ratio drops.

Our Workshop Operations Audit evaluates the physical and systemic lifecycle of every vehicle entering the service department. By cross-reconciling DMS timestamps, security logs, technician job clocking, and floor WIP reports, we identify bottlenecks, unauthorized delays, and operational non-compliance.

Strategic Imperative

Why Dealerships Need This Audit

Workshops leak millions annually through unbilled technician labor hours, bay bottlenecks, ghost job cards, excessive turnaround time (TAT), and unapproved sublet repairs. Without independent shop-floor scrutiny, Dealer Principals cannot identify whether workshop capacity is genuinely maximized or artificially choked by poor technician scheduling and unrecorded rework.

Common Ground Blind Spots Uncovered:
  • Technicians performing unbilled diagnostic and repair operations without repair order (RO) entry.
  • Bay idle time and technician downtime masking true workshop capacity.
  • Ghost job cards opened to manipulate OEM vehicle throughput statistics.
  • Sublet repairs sent to outside vendors without markup or authorization.
Comprehensive Scope

What We Audit

  • Job Card Verification: Complete lifecycle testing from physical opening to final digital closure.
  • Open & Pending Job Cards: Ageing analysis of WIP vehicles (same day, 1–2 days, 3–5 days, and >5 days).
  • Labour Operation Verification: Standard flat-rate hours vs actual hours booked on RO line-items.
  • Technician Productivity: Billed hours vs available hours, clocking gaps, and efficiency ratios.
  • Bay Utilisation: Stalled bays, mechanical vs bodyshop bay turnover, and idling patterns.
  • Vehicle Delivery TAT: Gap between promised delivery time, actual completion, and gate-out.
  • Repeat Repair Analysis: Vehicles returning within 30/60 days for identical failure codes.
  • Free Service Verification: OEM coupon validation, mandatory checks compliance, and zero-billing checks.
  • Process SOP Compliance: Floor road-testing logs, quality controller (QC) sign-offs, and final inspection checklist.
Risk Exposure

Common Operational Risks & Red Flags

Operational Vulnerabilities Uncovered in Field Audits:

  • Phantom Job Cards: ROs opened in DMS to claim OEM targets or inflate throughput, then cancelled or left pending indefinitely.
  • Aged Stalled WIP: Customer vehicles stranded for weeks (or even >100 days) due to unmanaged parts backorders or insurance surveyor disputes.
  • Technician Ticket Padding: Manual labor clocking assigned to senior technicians for work actually executed by apprentices.
  • Unbilled Supplementary Work: Technicians performing repairs identified on the floor without advisor estimate revision or customer billing.

Step-by-Step

Our Operational Audit Procedure

1
Population Completeness

Extract all opened ROs during the audit period and verify continuous sequence against cancellation logs.

2
WIP Physical Count

Conduct an on-site physical count of vehicles physically present on bays and in the dealership yard, reconciling against DMS open ROs.

3
Labour Hour Matching

Match technician clocking timestamps against standard flat-rate times defined by the OEM service manual.

4
TAT Band Stratification

Segment all closed repair orders into delivery turnaround bands (Same Day, 1–2 Days, 3–5 Days, >5 Days) to isolate long-stay outliers.

5
Root Cause Analysis

For all repair orders delayed beyond standard SLA, trace root cause back to parts backorders, customer approval delays, or advisor negligence.
