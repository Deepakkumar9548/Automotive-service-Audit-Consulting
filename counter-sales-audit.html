Counter Sales Audit

Forensic scrutiny of over-the-counter retail parts transactions, lubricants, batteries, tyres, accessories, unbilled sales, discount approvals, and cashier settlements.

Practice Overview

Securing Retail Parts Revenue & Eliminating OTC Pilferage

While workshop repair orders follow standard Job Card tracking, Over-The-Counter (OTC) retail sales operate under high cashflow velocity and direct customer interactions. Dealership parts counters generate substantial turnover through retail sales of genuine spare parts, engine oils, batteries, tyres, and car accessories to walk-in customers, local independent mechanics, and fleet buyers.

Because counter sales do not involve a physical vehicle entering the workshop bay, they are highly prone to control failures: cash sales pocketed without invoice generation, unauthorized trade discounts, unauthorized product substitutions, and fabricated sales return credit notes.

Our Counter Sales Audit provides end-to-end verification across 100% of retail transactions: comparing physical stock drawdowns against DMS billing, verifying cashier collections (Cash, UPI, POS Cards), inspecting trade credit limits, and plugging unbilled leakage.

Strategic Imperative

Why Dealerships Need This Audit

Over-the-counter retail sales (lubricants, batteries, tyres, consumables) operate outside workshop repair orders. Without strict controls, cash skimming, unbilled hand-delivery of parts to external garages, scrap battery diversion, and unauthorized wholesale discounts erode retail parts margin.

Common Ground Blind Spots Uncovered:
  • Cash collected at the retail counter without generating official DMS tax invoices.
  • Discrepancies between digital oil dispenser meters, drum dipsticks, and billed volumes.
  • Old customer battery scrap allowances diverted instead of credited to dealership accounts.
  • Unauthorized trade discounts and unapproved credit extended to local garages.
Comprehensive Scope

What We Audit

  • Counter Sales Verification: Complete review of retail cash memos and OTC tax invoices against store issuance logs.
  • Lubricants Sales Audit: Dispensed barrel litres vs billed quantities of engine oil, synthetic oil, gear oil, and coolant.
  • Battery Sales Audit: Serial number tracking, scrap old battery buyback credit verification, and warranty card validation.
  • Tyre Sales Audit: DOT date code verification, physical tyre rack counts vs DMS billing, and mounting fee reconciliation.
  • Accessories Sales Audit: Physical stock reconciliation of high-value car accessories, infotainment systems, seat covers, and mats.
  • Filters & Consumables Check: High-turnover fast-moving items (oil filters, air filters, brake pads) usage vs billing audit.
  • DMS vs Counter Reconciliation: Cross-verifying counter cash registers against DMS parts revenue entries and tax reports.
  • Cash, UPI & Credit Reconciliation: Verifying that every counter sale payment was deposited in full into authorized bank accounts.
  • Discount & Approval Verification: Scrutinizing trade discounts given to external garages against written management matrices.
  • Unbilled Sales Identification: Catching parts handed across the counter with manual challans or hand slips without billing.
  • Physical Stock vs System Stock: Wall-to-wall counts of OTC fast-moving racks comparing physical counts to system ledger.
  • Sales Return & Cancellation Checks: Auditing credit memos issued for returned parts to ensure physical parts re-entered stock.
  • Customer Contact Integrity: Confirming genuine retail customer names and GSTINs on high-value trade counter invoices.
  • Revenue Leakage Root Causes: Quantifying margin loss from unauthorized wholesale pricing, freight leakages, and unrecovered taxes.
Risk Exposure

Common Counter Sales Vulnerabilities

Critical Counter Sales Red Flags Discovered in Field Audits:

  • Cash Skimming at Counter: Parts handed to cash walk-in customers on unofficial quote slips; cash collected directly and inventory shortfall written off as "damage/loss" at month-end.
  • Lubricant Barrel Shortages: Unexplained 50 to 100 litre shortfalls in 208-litre lube drums due to unmetered retail can dispenses.
  • Old Battery Scrap Arbitrage: Dealership allowances granted for customer old batteries, but the scrap batteries are sold privately without crediting dealership accounts.
  • Fabricated Sales Returns: Invoices cancelled post-delivery under the pretext of "wrong part supplied", while parts were retained by the buyer.

Procedure

Our Counter Sales Audit Procedure

1
Full Sales Register Reconciliation

Extract 100% of counter sales records and verify sequential numbering to confirm no missing or deleted invoices.

2
Cash & Payment Triangulation

Match every counter invoice payment mode against bank statement credit lines and cashier daybook submissions.

3
Lubricant Dipstick Reconciliation

Measure physical barrel volumes vs DMS dispensed liters across all grades (5W-30, 0W-20, Coolant) to compute shrinkage.

4
Sales Return Physical Trace

Select 100% of high-value returned parts and verify physical existence in store bins or subsequent re-sale logs.

5
Discount Threshold Audit

Compare discounts granted on counter sales against authorized Delegation of Authority (DOA) matrices.
